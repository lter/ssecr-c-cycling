Fig1_conceptual_trajectories
----------------------------
Conceptual trajectories of treatment effects on carbon cycling over time, and
the inference errors that short-term observation windows can produce. Four
hypothetical response types are shown (stable, variable, increasing,
decreasing), all indistinguishable near zero effect during the short-term
window (shaded region, first three years). Extrapolating from the short-term
window alone yields an accurate estimate only for stable responses; it
underestimates responses that strengthen over time (increasing), overestimates
responses that attenuate or reverse (decreasing), and gives an uncertain
estimate for oscillating (variable) responses. Colors match the trend
classification used throughout the analysis.

Fig4_all_experiments_lrr_pooled_by_trend
----------------------------------------
Log response ratios (LRR; ln of treatment/control) over time for all 18
experiments. In each site panel, gray lines and points show individual
treatments; colored curves are loess fits pooled by temporal trend
classification (stable, variable, increasing, decreasing), with 95%
confidence bands. The dashed horizontal line at 0 indicates no treatment
effect. Bottom row: summary of the n = 83 classified treatment-experiment
combinations — proportions in each trend class (stable 35, variable 22,
increasing 21, decreasing 5), and distributions of the mean treatment/control
ratio (log scale), coefficient of variation (dotted line at the CV = 0.3
classification threshold), and experiment duration by trend class.
Confidence bands are omitted where a site has too few timepoints to
estimate smoother uncertainty (ARC, NTL).


Fig5_detection_time_analysis
----------------------------
Factors influencing the time required to detect statistically significant
temporal trends in treatment response ratios. Only treatment-experiment
combinations classified as increasing (blue; n = 21) or decreasing (red;
n = 5) are included. (A) Distribution of years required to first achieve
statistical significance (P < 0.05 in sequential linear regression) by trend
direction. (B) Relationship between measurement density (observations per
year) and detection time; densely sampled experiments do not consistently
detect trends faster. (C) Total study duration versus the number of
timepoints required for detection, with bubble size proportional to
measurement density, illustrating the interplay between calendar time,
sampling effort, and detectability. (D) Detection time as a function of
absolute slope magnitude (log scale); steeper trends are detected sooner.
(E) Detection time versus the coefficient of variation of the
treatment/control ratio; higher temporal variability delays detection.
(F) Detection time by LTER site, showing cross-site variation in how quickly
significant trends emerged. Trend lines in panels B, D, and E are linear
fits with 95% confidence bands.


Fig6_early_vs_full_lrr
----------------------
Comparison of early versus full-duration log-response ratios (LRR) across
treatment-experiment combinations (n = 80). Each point represents a single
treatment within a long-term experiment, with the mean LRR calculated over the
first three years of observation (x-axis) plotted against the mean LRR over the
full study duration (y-axis). The dashed diagonal line indicates 1:1
correspondence; points above the line indicate treatments whose effects
strengthened over time, and points below indicate treatments whose effects
attenuated. Colors and shapes denote LTER site identity. Most treatments
cluster near the origin and the 1:1 line (Pearson r = 0.68), indicating that
early-period effect sizes are broadly predictive of long-term outcomes, though
substantial scatter — particularly among treatments with strong initial
effects — suggests that short-term results can both overestimate and
underestimate long-term responses.


Fig7_sizer_and_sign_flips
-------------------------
Piecewise slope-change (SiZer) analysis and treatment response sign-flip
frequency across LTER sites. (A) One representative treatment per site
(selected as the treatment with the most timepoints; 16 sites with at least 5
observations), with the treatment/control ratio plotted over time. Piecewise
linear segments are derived from SiZer analysis at a 5-year smoothing
bandwidth: blue segments indicate slopes significantly different from zero;
grey segments indicate slopes indistinguishable from zero. Vertical dotted
lines mark detected slope-change boundaries. Grey ribbons show plus/minus 1 SE
of the mean treatment/control ratio, and the dashed horizontal line marks a
ratio of 1 (no treatment effect). Of 79 treatment combinations analyzed (those with at least 5 timepoints), 23
(29%) exhibited at least one statistically significant slope change.
(B) Sign-flip frequency — the percentage of consecutive measurement
transitions in which the treatment response crossed the no-effect threshold
(ratio = 1) — across all classified treatments at each site (n = 83
treatments across 18 sites), displayed as horizontal boxplots with individual
values overlaid. Expressing flips as a percentage of transitions normalizes
for differences in study duration and measurement frequency across sites.
Colors indicate ecosystem type. Sign-flip frequency varied widely both within
and among sites (mean = 20%, range: 0-67%), reflecting heterogeneous temporal
dynamics of treatment effects.


FigS1_trend_characteristics_scatter
-----------------------------------
Characteristics of the temporal trend classification. Each point is one
treatment-experiment combination, plotted by its coefficient of variation
(x-axis) and the slope of the linear fit of the treatment/control ratio over
time (y-axis), colored by assigned trend class. Dotted lines mark the
classification thresholds: combinations with a significant slope (P < 0.05)
are classified increasing or decreasing by slope sign; non-significant
combinations are classified stable (CV < 0.3) or variable (CV >= 0.3).
