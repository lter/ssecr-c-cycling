early_vs_full_lrr
-----------------
Comparison of early versus full-duration log-response ratios (LRR) across
treatment-experiment combinations (n = 79). Each point represents a single
treatment within a long-term experiment, with the mean LRR calculated over the
first three years of observation (x-axis) plotted against the mean LRR over the
full study duration (y-axis). The dashed diagonal line indicates 1:1
correspondence; points above the line indicate treatments whose effects
strengthened over time, and points below indicate treatments whose effects
attenuated. Colors and shapes denote LTER site identity. Most treatments cluster
near the origin and the 1:1 line, indicating that early-period effect sizes are
broadly predictive of long-term outcomes, though substantial scatter --
particularly among treatments with strong initial effects -- suggests that
short-term results can both overestimate and underestimate long-term responses.


detection_time_analysis
-----------------------
Factors influencing the time required to detect statistically significant
temporal trends in treatment response ratios. Only treatment-experiment
combinations classified as increasing (green; n = 18) or decreasing (red; n = 5)
are included. (A) Distribution of years required to first achieve statistical
significance (P < 0.05 in sequential linear regression) by trend direction.
(B) Relationship between measurement density (observations per year) and
detection time; densely sampled experiments do not consistently detect trends
faster. (C) Total study duration versus the number of timepoints required for
detection, with bubble size proportional to measurement density, illustrating
the interplay between calendar time, sampling effort, and detectability.
(D) Detection time as a function of absolute slope magnitude (log scale);
steeper trends are detected sooner. (E) Detection time versus the coefficient of
variation of the treatment/control ratio; higher temporal variability delays
detection. (F) Detection time by LTER site, showing cross-site variation in how
quickly significant trends emerged. Trend lines in panels B, D, and E are linear
fits with 95% confidence bands.


Figure_sizer_and_sign_flips
---------------------------
Piecewise slope-change (SiZer) analysis and treatment response sign-flip
frequency across LTER sites. (A) One representative treatment per site (selected
as the treatment with the most timepoints; 16 sites with at least 5
observations), with the treatment/control ratio plotted over time. Piecewise
linear segments are derived from SiZer analysis at a 5-year smoothing bandwidth:
blue segments indicate slopes significantly different from zero; grey segments
indicate slopes indistinguishable from zero. Vertical dotted lines mark detected
slope-change boundaries. Grey ribbons show plus/minus 1 SE of the mean
treatment/control ratio, and the dashed horizontal line marks a ratio of 1 (no
treatment effect). Of 78 treatment combinations analyzed, 23 (29%) exhibited at
least one statistically significant slope change. (B) Sign-flip frequency -- the percentage of consecutive measurement transitions
in which the treatment response crossed the no-effect threshold (ratio = 1) --
across all classified treatments at each site (n = 80 treatments across 17
sites), displayed as horizontal boxplots with individual values overlaid.
Expressing flips as a percentage of transitions normalizes for differences in
study duration and measurement frequency across sites. Colors indicate ecosystem
type. Sign-flip frequency varied widely both within and among sites (mean = 21%,
range: 0-67%), reflecting heterogeneous temporal dynamics of treatment effects.
